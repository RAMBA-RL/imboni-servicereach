How this tool works

Data sources

  • Population: 2022 Census (NISR), mapped with WorldPop 2020.
  • Poverty: census poverty index by sector (NISR), checked with the EICV7 survey.
  • Facilities and roads: public facility list (Maina et al. 2019) and OpenStreetMap.

How priority is calculated

  • We estimate the walk from every 100 m cell to the nearest facility: 5 km/h on roads and paths, 2.5 km/h elsewhere.
  • We count people more than 60 minutes away, then multiply by a poverty rate.
  • Check: our average walk is 29.8 minutes. The EICV7 survey says 31.7.

Different poverty definitions and different map detail give different totals. We use the sector measure because it is more local. The top sectors stay the same in most cases: 17 of the top 20 are confirmed by both.

National priorities

Supports NST2 (2024–2029), Social Transformation pillar: stronger health systems and graduation from poverty. Supports Vision 2050: quality of life for all Rwandans.

Keeping it up to date

DataDateHow it is updated
PopulationCensus 2022When NISR publishes new census or projections
PovertyCensus 2022, EICV7 (2023/24)When NISR publishes a new survey
Health facilitiesPublic list 2019, OpenStreetMapNew facilities are added as a row in a file, or pulled from OpenStreetMap with a script. Then the analysis is rerun

This is a planning tool, not a real-time system. Poverty and population come from surveys and censuses, so they change only when NISR releases new data. Rerunning takes minutes.

Limitations

  • Health posts are only partly mapped.
  • Hills are not modelled, and walking speeds are averages. A shorter walk does not mean care is available, and a site may not be buildable.
  • Poverty is assumed even inside a sector. The new-site tool tests one site at a time, on a 2 km grid.
  • Results are for planning discussion only.
